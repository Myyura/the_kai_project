## **Description**
### Q.1
Consider the system

$$
\begin{cases}
x+2y+z=3,\\
ay+4z=10,\\
2x+6y+az=b.
\end{cases}
$$

(1) Find those values of $a$ for which the system has a unique solution.

(2) Find those pairs of values $(a,b)$ for which the system has more than one solution.

### Q.2
Consider the matrix

$$
A=
\begin{pmatrix}
3&1&-1\\
1&3&-1\\
1&1&1
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

(1) Find all eigenvalues of $A$.

(2) Find a maximum set $S$ of linearly independent eigenvectors of $A$.

(3) Determine whether $A$ is diagonalizable. If it is, find $P$ such that
$D=P^{-1}AP$ is diagonal; otherwise, prove that it is not diagonalizable.

### 题目描述
1. 对方程组

   $$
   \begin{cases}
   x+2y+z=3,\\
   ay+4z=10,\\
   2x+6y+az=b
   \end{cases}
   $$

   回答：
   1. 求使方程组有唯一解的 $a$；
   2. 求使方程组有多于一个解的 $(a,b)$。
2. 对矩阵

   $$
   A=\begin{pmatrix}3&1&-1\\1&3&-1\\1&1&1\end{pmatrix},
   $$

   求全部特征值、线性无关特征向量的最大集合，并判断是否可对角化；若可，求
   $P$ 使 $P^{-1}AP$ 为对角矩阵。

## **Kai**
### Q.1
The coefficient determinant is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\det
\begin{pmatrix}
1&2&1\\
0&a&4\\
2&6&a
\end{pmatrix}
&=a^2-2a-8\\
&=(a-4)(a+2).
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence the system has a unique solution exactly when

$$
\boxed{a\ne4,-2}.
$$

For more than one solution, the coefficient matrix must be singular and the
augmented system must be consistent.

- If $a=4$, the third coefficient row satisfies
  $R_3=2R_1+\frac12R_2$. Thus consistency requires
  $b=2\cdot3+\frac12\cdot10=11$.
- If $a=-2$, the third coefficient row satisfies
  $R_3=2R_1-R_2$. Thus consistency requires
  $b=2\cdot3-10=-4$.

Therefore,

$$
\boxed{(a,b)=(4,11)\ \text{or}\ (-2,-4)}.
$$

In either case the consistent singular system has infinitely many solutions.

### Q.2
#### (1)
The characteristic polynomial is

$$
\det(\lambda I-A)=(\lambda-2)^2(\lambda-3).
$$

Thus the eigenvalues are

$$
\boxed{\lambda=2\ \text{(multiplicity 2)},\qquad \lambda=3}.
$$

#### (2)
For $\lambda=2$,

$$
(A-2I)\boldsymbol{x}=0
\iff x+y-z=0,
$$

so its eigenspace has dimension $2$. For $\lambda=3$, the eigenspace is
$\operatorname{span}\{(1,1,1)^\mathsf{T}\}$. One maximum linearly independent
set is therefore

$$
\boxed{
S=\left\{
\begin{pmatrix}1\\-1\\0\end{pmatrix},
\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\\1\end{pmatrix},
\begin{pmatrix}1\\1\\1\end{pmatrix}
\right\}.}
$$

#### (3)
The three vectors in $S$ are linearly independent. Taking them as columns gives

$$
P=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&1&1\\
-1&0&1\\
0&1&1
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \det P=-1\ne0.
$$

Consequently,

$$
\boxed{P^{-1}AP=
\begin{pmatrix}
2&0&0\\
0&2&0\\
0&0&3
\end{pmatrix}.}
$$

Thus $A$ is diagonalizable.
